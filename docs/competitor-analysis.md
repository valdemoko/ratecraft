# Competitor Analysis — RateCraft

> Research date: September 14, 2026. Based on SERP observation and public page inspection.
> No content, design, or branding copied — this analysis identifies gaps to fill differently.

## Summary of patterns

**What almost everyone does:**

- Calculators are lead magnets for paid FSM/construction software (Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Buildertrend, busybusy…).
- Calculator pages are thin: 2–4 inputs, one output, minimal explanation, no formula transparency.
- Content is produced to sell software: "what is X" posts with heavy CTAs.
- Design is either generic SaaS (rounded cards, purple/blue gradients) or ad-heavy directories.

**What almost nobody does:**

- Show the formulas behind results.
- Connect tools into a chain (burden → job cost → price → margin).
- Address the markup-vs-margin confusion inside the tool itself.
- Treat trades users with trades-specific inputs (burden %, workers' comp, non-billable hours).

## What they do well (worth learning, not copying)

1. Tool pages that load instantly and require no signup (busybusy sets the standard).
2. One clear job per tool — no feature creep.
3. Related-search coverage: they capture long-tail PAA questions in FAQ sections.

## What they do badly (our openings)

1. **No math transparency** — results appear by magic; formulas never shown.
2. **No chain between tools** — labor burden output should feed job cost input; nowhere does this.
3. **Markup/margin confusion left unresolved** — most pages repeat it or ignore it.
4. **Generic inputs** — "revenue" and "COGS" instead of burdened labor, subs, dump fees.
5. **Lead-gen UX** — email gates, demo CTAs, exit intent.
6. **Weak E-E-A-T** — no methodology pages, no limitations, no correction channels.

## UX / speed / mobile observations

- busybusy & ServiceTitan tools: fast, mobile-fine, but cramped on small screens.
- Housecall Pro: interstitials and popups degrade mobile UX.
- Many blog competitors: 3–6 MB pages, layout shift from ads, slow LCP.
- Opportunity: genuinely mobile-first calculators with large touch targets and no popups.

## Advertising observed

- Blog competitors run AdSense/display ads mid-content (often 3–5 slots per article).
- Tool pages are mostly ad-free (lead-gen instead). Our model (clean tools, modest in-content
  ads on guide/article pages only) has no strong adversarial precedent.

## Conclusion for positioning

There is a real hole for a **tool-first, math-transparent, trades-native** pricing platform
that links calculators into one decision chain and pairs each tool with a genuinely good
guide. No observed competitor combines all four.
